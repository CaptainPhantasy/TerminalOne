## LONG-FORM (The Manifesto We'd Put On A Wall)

---

### We Built A Terminal Because Someone Charged For One

Here is a list of things that predate the terminal emulator as a software category: the graphical user interface, the computer mouse, the laser printer, and approximately 60% of the people currently selling you software subscriptions.

The terminal is the oldest tool in the box. It is a box you type into and the computer types back. That's the whole thing. We've been doing it since before "user experience" was a phrase anyone said in a meeting.

And yet.

Somehow, in the year of whatever year this is — somewhere around 2026, we stopped counting — the software industry arrived at a place where a terminal emulator costs money every month. Not once. Every month. Forever. A recurring extraction for the privilege of typing commands into a box.

We live in a garage in Brown County, Indiana. We have two cats. We have coffee that has transcended its original purpose. We have opinions about this.

TerminalOne is what happened when those opinions met a code editor at 2:47 AM.

**38 features.** Not "38 features with more behind a paywall." Thirty-eight features, all of them, forever. Multi-session tabs. Regex search. Clipboard bridges for both OSC 52 and OSC 777. Pinch-to-zoom. A configurable key bar that actually configures. Themes that respect system dark mode. Autosave that saves your scrollback so when your browser crashes — and it will, it's a browser — you don't lose the thing you were reading. A status bar that tells you latency, uptime, and shell type because you deserve to know how your own terminal is feeling.

**One port.** 11001. The server and the WebSocket share it. No cross-origin gymnastics. No "did you open the right port" conversation with yourself at 3 AM.

**Zero dollars.** Not a trial. Not a "free tier." The whole thing. We're not renting you a terminal. We're giving you one. The way computing worked before someone invented the subscription treadmill and convinced everyone it was a feature.

**One command.** `t1`. Two keystrokes. If you're going to open a terminal, you shouldn't have to type a paragraph to get there.

Bella — our Senior Quality Assurance engineer, a twenty-pound black cat — walked across the keyboard during testing. She switched sessions. She opened the command palette. She did not subscribe to anything.

We took that as approval.

TerminalOne. Welcome to the machine.

It's free. It was always supposed to be.
